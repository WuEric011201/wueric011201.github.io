Woodshop

A walnut box, a folding stool, and some plywood.

I spend most of my working life on things that are 28 nanometres wide and only exist on a screen until a fab sends them back months later. The woodshop is the opposite of that in every way, which is exactly why I like it.

Walnut keepsake box

Walnut, oil finish, antique brass clasp.
The whole design fits on one sheet of paper. Stock thickness sets everything else, so the arithmetic gets done before anything is cut.

The lesson that transfers: every dimension is a subtraction from something you already cut. Walnut is not cheap and there is no undo, so the sketch on the right of that page is really a constraint solver — outer dimension, wall thickness, and the rabbet depth for the lid all have to close before the saw is switched on.

Brass hinges let into the lid, and a velvet-lined interior with a divider.

Folding stool

Open and flat. Pine, a dowel pivot, and no hardware.

A folding stool is a mechanism, not a piece of furniture. The two leg frames pivot on a single dowel and the whole thing has to be loose enough to fold and tight enough not to rack when someone sits on it. Wood moves with humidity, so a clearance that is right in the shop can be wrong in a living room — the fit is a design decision, not a fabrication tolerance.
